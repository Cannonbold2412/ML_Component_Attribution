\begin{abstract}
Hybrid trading systems combine a rule-based strategy with machine-learned components, yet evaluations rarely identify
the decision at which machine learning (ML) adds value. We introduce matched-budget component attribution (MBCA), a
controlled-variable protocol that holds data, features, model class, hyperparameters, freeze discipline, and
evaluation fixed while replacing one insertion point at a time---regime filter, meta-labeling, position sizing, entry,
or exit---with a learned counterpart. Study~1 applies MBCA to one strategy in three markets and finds \SoneSigGain{}
significant improvements and \SoneSigLoss{} significant losses in \SoneConfigs{} configurations. Study~2,
pre-registered in version control before any of its data existed, applies MBCA to five historically documented
strategies on \STwoInstruments{} daily series in \STwoMarkets{} asset-class markets, with two independent freeze
dates, close-price stop fills, per-market costs, mark-to-market portfolios, a paired block bootstrap, and
false-discovery-rate control. Of \STwoMktTests{} market-level tests, \STwoMktRawSig{} have a raw $p<\STwoAlpha$, but
only \STwoMktSigGain{} survive as significant gains and \STwoMktSigLoss{} as significant losses. Narrowing components
(meta-labeling, sizing) improve the Sharpe ratio in \STwoMktMetaPos{} and \STwoMktSizingPos{} of cells, are never
significantly harmful, and transfer across markets, but their effects are small and replicate only partially across
freezes. Replacing components are strategy dependent: ML entry is the most harmful component, whereas ML exit is the
only one that significantly improves a strategy in several markets (RSI(2) mean reversion, \STwoMktExitGainRSI{}
significant gains). Execution assumptions dominate every ML effect: stop-level fills inflate the controls' Sharpe ratios
by \STwoExecBaseStopMin{} to \STwoExecBaseStopMax{}.
\end{abstract}
