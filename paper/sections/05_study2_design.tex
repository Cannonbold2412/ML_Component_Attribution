\section{Study 2: Design}
\label{sec:study2design}

\subsection{Pre-Registration and a Single Source of Truth}

Every Study~2 design choice---universe, cleaning rules, strategies and parameter grids, components, freeze dates,
execution model, statistics, regime definitions, and crisis windows---is declared in one configuration file that was
committed to version control \emph{before} any Study~2 data were downloaded or any result existed. The implementation
reads nothing else: the same file drives data collection, every experiment, and every table, figure, and in-text
number of this section and the next, all of which are generated by the pipeline rather than typed
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). The only deviation from the pre-registration was forced by data availability and is
logged: one planned symbol (Roche, \texttt{ROG.SW}) was not served by the data source, leaving \STwoInstruments{} of
\STwoPlanned{} planned series. The regime-filter component was restricted in advance to a single label definition.

\subsection{Data and Provenance}

Daily open, high, low, and close prices for \STwoInstruments{} instruments in \STwoMarkets{} markets
(Table~\ref{tab:s2data}, Fig.~\ref{fig:universe}) were downloaded from the Yahoo Finance chart interface on
\STwoFetched{}, covering \STwoFirst{} to \STwoLast{} and \STwoRows{} daily bars after cleaning. The universe comprises
\STwoNequityindices{} equity indices from the Americas, Europe, and Asia-Pacific; \STwoNequityetfs{} US sector and
country ETFs; \STwoNfixedincome{} bond ETFs and Treasury futures; \STwoNcommodities{} commodity futures;
\STwoNfx{} currency pairs, including emerging-market currencies; \STwoNcrypto{} cryptocurrencies; and
\STwoNstocksus{}~US and \STwoNstocksintl{} non-US large-capitalization stocks listed on \STwoExchanges{} exchanges.
The raw responses are stored byte for byte with their URL, UTC fetch time, and SHA-256 hash, and cleaning is a
deterministic function of the raw bytes and the declared rules: split and dividend adjustment via the adjusted close,
removal of \STwoDropped{} rows with missing or non-positive prices, \STwoRepaired{} OHLC-consistency repairs, and
\STwoBadTicks{} reversing bad ticks.

Two independent checks validate the data. Against \STwoFredN{} FRED series, five-day return correlations range from
\STwoFredCorrFiveMin{} to \STwoFredCorrFiveMax{} and the median absolute price difference is at most
\STwoFredMedDiffMax{}; daily FX correlations are lower only because the two sources fix FX at different times of day,
and front-month natural-gas futures, a different instrument from Henry Hub spot, correlate at \STwoNgCorrFive{}. A
browser spot-check of \STwoSpotDays{} closing prices for \STwoSpotSymbols{} symbols against MarketWatch finds a
maximum difference of \STwoSpotMaxDiff{}.

\subsection{The Five Strategies}

Five historically documented rule families serve as controls (Table~\ref{tab:s2strat}): the JMA crossover of Study~1;
the dual moving-average crossover of Brock, Lakonishok, and LeBaron \cite{brock1992simple}; the Donchian channel
breakout of the Turtle rules \cite{faith2007turtle}; time-series momentum \cite{moskowitz2012momentum}, entered when
the sign of the trailing six- or twelve-month return changes; and RSI(2) short-term mean reversion in the direction of
the 200-day trend \cite{connors2008short}. Four are trend-following and one is contrarian, and their holding periods
range from days to months. Each control's parameters are walk-forward selected per instrument exactly as in Study~1.

\subsection{Execution and Portfolio Accounting}

Execution is deliberately conservative. Signals and exits are evaluated on daily closes; a stop breached by a close is
filled \emph{at that close}, not at the stop level (the optimistic stop-level fill of Study~1 is re-run as a
sensitivity), and entries fill at the signal bar's close (a one-day entry lag is re-run as a sensitivity). The
round-trip transaction cost plus slippage is set per market in advance---\STwoCostfx{}\,bps for FX,
\STwoCostequityindices{}\,bps for indices, ETFs, and futures, \STwoCoststocksus{}\,bps for single stocks, and
\STwoCostcrypto{}\,bps for crypto---and varied by the multipliers \STwoCostMults{}. Performance is measured on
\emph{mark-to-market} daily portfolios: capital is split equally across the instruments of a market (or of the whole
universe) that trade on a given day, each position earns its instrument's close-to-close return from entry to exit,
and half of the round-trip cost is charged at entry and half at exit, scaled by position size. Sharpe and Sortino
ratios use no risk-free rate, because the universe spans many currencies.

\subsection{Windows, Generalization, and Robustness Design}

Two independent freezes give two non-overlapping out-of-sample windows, each with its own frozen models
(Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}): F\STwoFzA{}, with models trained on \STwoStart--\STwoFzATrainEnd{} and tested on
\STwoFzA--\STwoFzAEnd{}, a window that includes the global financial crisis; and F\STwoFzB{}, trained on
\STwoStart--\STwoFzBTrainEnd{} and tested from \STwoFzB{} onward, including the COVID crash and the 2022 bear market.
Crypto enters only F\STwoFzB{}. Each (strategy, market, freeze) comparison is a \emph{market-level} test, and the
equal-weight portfolio of all \STwoInstruments{} instruments gives a \emph{global} test per (strategy, freeze).

Beyond the main grid, the design pre-registers the following checks: \emph{cross-market transfer}, in which
meta-labeling and meta-label + sizing models are fit on every other market and applied to the held-out one;
\emph{budget ablations}, with meta-label thresholds of \STwoThrLo{} and \STwoThrHi{} and a small model of
\STwoSmallTrees{} trees of depth \STwoSmallDepth{} for meta-labeling and exit; \emph{execution} and
\emph{transaction-cost} sensitivities; \emph{cross-regime} tests, with bull and bear regimes defined by the market
index's position relative to its 200-day average and high- and low-volatility regimes by 20-day realized volatility
relative to its expanding median, both known at $t-1$; five crisis windows; a \emph{survivorship} split between
survivorship-free indices, ETFs, futures, and FX and current-constituent stocks and surviving coins;
leave-one-instrument-out and leave-one-market-out jackknives; and an in-sample versus out-of-sample decay check of the
controls' parameter selection.

\subsection{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

The difference in annualized Sharpe ratio between a variant and its control is tested with a paired circular-block
bootstrap \cite{politis1992circular,ledoit2008robust}. Both daily return series are aligned on the same dates, and
the \emph{same} \STwoBlock-day blocks are resampled for both in each of \STwoBoot{} replicates, which preserves their
cross-correlation and short-range serial dependence and corrects the unpaired i.i.d.\ test of Study~1. The two-sided
$p$-value is $2\min\{P(\Delta^{*} \leq 0), P(\Delta^{*} \geq 0)\}$, where $\Delta^{*}$ is the bootstrap replicate of
$\Delta\mathrm{SR}$. Multiplicity is controlled with the Benjamini--Hochberg FDR \cite{benjamini1995fdr} within
pre-defined families (the \STwoMktTests{} market-level main tests, the \STwoGlobTests{} global main tests, and the
transfer, ablation, and execution families), with Holm's method \cite{holm1979simple} reported as a family-wise check
in the spirit of \cite{harvey2016cross,sullivan1999data}. A difference is called significant iff $q < \STwoAlpha$.
Deflated Sharpe ratios \cite{bailey2014deflated} are reported for every portfolio in the supplementary data.
