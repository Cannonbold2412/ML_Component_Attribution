\section{Study 2: Results}
\label{sec:study2results}

\subsection{Headline: few significant effects, and they are strategy-specific}

Of the \STwoMktTests{} pre-registered market-level main tests (7 components $\times$ 5 strategies
$\times$ 8 markets $\times$ 2 freezes, less cells without training data), \STwoMktRawSig{} have a raw
$p<\STwoAlpha$ --- far more than the $\approx5\%$ expected under a global null, so the ML components do
change performance --- but after Benjamini--Hochberg correction only \STwoMktSigGain{} are
significant gains and \STwoMktSigLoss{} significant losses (Holm: \STwoMktHolmSig{} of either sign).
\STwoMktPosShare{} of all market-level differences are positive. On the all-market portfolio, which
diversifies across \STwoInstruments{} instruments, \STwoGlobSigGain{} of the \STwoGlobTests{} global
tests is a significant gain and \STwoGlobSigLoss{} a significant loss (Table~\ref{tab:s2headline},
Figure~\ref{fig:s2forest}). No component significantly improves any of the four trend-following
strategies on the all-market portfolio in either freeze.

\subsection{Component attribution}

Table~\ref{tab:s2comp} and Figure~\ref{fig:s2heatmap} give the per-component picture, which
refines the taxonomy of Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Narrowing components are the most consistently positive and never significantly
        harmful.} Meta-labeling improves Sharpe in \STwoMktMetaPos{} of market-level cells (median
        \STwoMktMetaMed{}; \STwoMktMetaGain{} significant gain, \STwoMktMetaLoss{} losses), meta-label
        + sizing in \STwoMktBestPos{} (median \STwoMktBestMed{}), and position sizing in
        \STwoMktSizingPos{} (median \STwoMktSizingMed{}, no significant result either way). They also
        cut turnover (median $\Delta$ \STwoMktMetaTurn{} and \STwoMktBestTurn{} capital/year). Their
        gains are small relative to their uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{ML entry is the most harmful component.} It loses in most cells (median
        \STwoMktEntryMed{}; \STwoMktEntryLoss{} significant losses) and raises turnover by
        \STwoMktEntryTurn{} capital/year. The damage is concentrated in the contrarian strategy:
        \STwoMktEntryLossRSI{} of its significant losses are RSI(2) cells (median
        \STwoMktEntryMedRSI{}), where a learned trend-oriented entry replaces a mean-reversion rule.
  \item \textbf{ML exit is strategy-dependent, not uniformly harmful.} For the four trend-following
        controls its median effect is \STwoMktExitMedTrend{}, but for RSI(2) it is
        \STwoMktExitMedRSI{}, with \STwoMktExitGainRSI{} significant gains --- the only component to
        significantly improve a strategy in several markets (global F\STwoFzA{}:
        \STwoGRSIExitADelta{}, CI \STwoGRSIExitACI{}). RSI(2)'s fixed ATR profit target caps
        winners, and a learned continuation exit lets them run; for trend-followers, whose trailing
        stops already let winners run, the learned exit mostly adds turnover (median
        \STwoMktExitTurn{}).
  \item \textbf{Independent gates do nothing reliable.} The ML regime filter (median
        \STwoMktRegimeMed{}, \STwoMktRegimeLoss{} significant results) and the rule-based ADX gate
        (median \STwoMktAdxMed{}, \STwoMktAdxLoss{} significant loss) are indistinguishable from zero.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Before vs.\ after, in full}

Table~\ref{tab:s2headline} reports every global before/after comparison and
Table~\ref{tab:s2metrics} the full risk profile (CAGR, volatility, Sharpe, Sortino, maximum drawdown,
CVaR, turnover, skewness, trades). Two facts frame all ML effects. First, the rule-based controls are
weak out of sample: their all-market Sharpe ratios range from \STwoGRSIBaseA{} (RSI(2), F\STwoFzA) to
\STwoGJMABaseA{} (JMA, F\STwoFzA), and their walk-forward parameter selection decays sharply out of
sample (Table~\ref{tab:s2decay}; e.g.\ dual SMA crossover \STwoDecaySMAIs{} bps per trade in-sample
vs.\ \STwoDecaySMAOos{} out-of-sample, time-series momentum \STwoDecayTSMOMIs{} vs.\
\STwoDecayTSMOMOos{}). The historical edge of these rules is largely absent since \STwoFzA{} under
realistic fills and costs. Second, most drawdown changes are mechanical exposure effects rather than
skill: every component that trades less shallows the all-market maximum drawdown in most cases
(meta-labeling \STwoGlobMetaDD{}, meta-label + sizing \STwoGlobBestDD{}, ML exit \STwoGlobExitDD{},
ML regime filter \STwoGlobRegimeDD{}; median exposure change \STwoGlobMetaExp{}, \STwoGlobBestExp{},
\STwoGlobExitExp{}, \STwoGlobRegimeExp{}), whereas ML entry, which raises exposure by
\STwoGlobEntryExp{}, deepens it in every case (\STwoGlobEntryDD{} shallower). Risk-adjusted return
--- the Sharpe ratio --- is therefore the right yardstick for attribution.

\subsection{Replication across freezes}

The two freezes are independent replications with different training data and non-overlapping test
periods. Agreement is modest (Table~\ref{tab:s2comp}): the sign of a strategy $\times$ market effect
agrees across freezes in \STwoRepMetaAgree{} of cells for meta-labeling, \STwoRepBestAgree{} for
meta-label + sizing, \STwoRepExitAgree{} for exit and \STwoRepEntryAgree{} for entry, but only
\STwoRepRegimeAgree{} and \STwoRepSizingAgree{} --- no better than chance --- for the regime filter and
position sizing (cross-freeze correlations of $\Delta$Sharpe: exit \STwoRepExitCorr{}, entry
\STwoRepEntryCorr{}, meta-labeling \STwoRepMetaCorr{}, sizing \STwoRepSizingCorr{}). The components
with large effects (entry, exit) replicate best; the small positive effects of the narrowing
components replicate only partially.

\subsection{Generalization across markets}

Models fit on every \emph{other} market and applied to a held-out one do at least as well as models fit
in-market: transferred meta-labeling has median $\Delta$Sharpe \STwoTransferMetaMed{} (positive in
\STwoTransferMetaPos{} of cells, \STwoTransferMetaLoss{} significant losses) and transferred meta-label
+ sizing \STwoTransferBestMed{} (\STwoTransferBestPos{}), vs.\ \STwoMktMetaMed{} and
\STwoMktBestMed{} in-market. Whatever the meta-label model learns is not market-specific.

\subsection{Robustness, stress, and sensitivity}

\textbf{Execution assumptions matter more than any ML component.} Filling a breached stop at the stop
level instead of at the breaching close --- the Study 1 convention --- raises the rule-based control's
all-market Sharpe by \STwoExecBaseStopMin{} to \STwoExecBaseStopMax{} (significant in
\STwoExecBaseStopSig{} strategy $\times$ freeze cases). Under that optimistic fill, ML exit looks far
worse (median \STwoExecExitStopMed{}, \STwoExecExitStopSig{} significant) than under realistic fills (median \STwoGlobExitMed{}),
because the control, not the exit, is flattered; part of Study 1's ``ML exit is harmful'' finding is
therefore an artifact of optimistic stop fills. A one-day entry lag changes the control by a median
\STwoExecBaseLagMed{} and leaves the ML comparisons qualitatively unchanged.

\textbf{Costs.} The narrowing components are insensitive to transaction costs (meta-label + sizing:
median global $\Delta$Sharpe \STwoCostBestZero{}, \STwoCostBestOne{}, \STwoCostBestTwo{},
\STwoCostBestFour{} at cost $\times$0, 1, 2, 4) because they reduce turnover; ML exit's effect decays from
\STwoCostExitZero{} to \STwoCostExitFour{} (Figure~\ref{fig:s2costs}).

\textbf{Budget ablations.} Moving the meta-label threshold to \STwoThrLo{} or \STwoThrHi{} (median \STwoAblThrMed{},
\STwoAblThrHiMed{}) or shrinking the model to \STwoSmallTrees{} trees of depth \STwoSmallDepth{} (meta-labeling \STwoAblMetaSmallMed{};
exit \STwoAblExitSmallMed{}) leaves the conclusions unchanged: effects come from the insertion point,
not from the particular budget.

\textbf{Regimes and crises.} Narrowing components help in low-volatility regimes (meta-labeling
\STwoRegMetaLowVol{}, meta-label + sizing \STwoRegBestLowVol{}) and slightly hurt in high-volatility
regimes (\STwoRegMetaHighVol{}, \STwoRegBestHighVol{}); ML exit helps in bear markets
(\STwoRegExitBear{}) and hurts in bull markets (\STwoRegExitBull{}), consistent with cutting trades
early (Tables~\ref{tab:s2reg}, \ref{tab:s2crisis}).

\textbf{Survivorship, instruments, markets.} Effects in survivorship-free markets and in
current-constituent stocks and crypto are similar for the narrowing components (meta-label + sizing
median \STwoSurvBestFree{} vs.\ \STwoSurvBestProne{}; meta-labeling \STwoSurvMetaFree{} vs.\
\STwoSurvMetaProne{}) and small for exit (\STwoSurvExitFree{} vs.\ \STwoSurvExitProne{}), so survivorship
does not drive the conclusions. Dropping any single instrument flips the sign of \STwoJackFlipShare{} of market-level
effects --- most are fragile --- but none of the \STwoMktSigGain{}$+$\STwoMktSigLoss{} significant ones
(\STwoJackSigFlip{} flip); dropping any single market flips \STwoJackMarketFlipShare{} of global effects.
