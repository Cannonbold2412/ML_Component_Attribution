\section{Study 1: One Strategy, Three Markets}
\label{sec:study1}

\subsection{Design}

The control is a JMA(7)/JMA(21) crossover whose parameters are walk-forward selected from a small
grid, with a fixed $\SoneSl\times$ATR stop / $\SoneTp\times$ATR target (forex) or a $\SoneSl\times$ATR stop with a
ratcheting trailing stop (commodities, Indian equities). Markets: a 4-pair forex sleeve (AUDNZD,
CADJPY, AUDUSD, NZDUSD), five commodities (WTI, Brent, natural gas, gold, silver), and 48 NIFTY-50
stocks with an annual sector-curation overlay. Each market's models were frozen at 70\% of its
calendar span; results are reported on a \emph{holdout} window after the freeze and, for commodities
and Indian equities, on a \emph{forward} window of data fetched after the study was designed. Study 1
reports a trade stream as a daily series by averaging the returns of trades exiting each day, Sharpe
ratios annualized with a 6\%/yr risk-free rate, and a 95\% CI from 2{,}000 \emph{unpaired} i.i.d.\
bootstrap resamples of each variant's daily series, computed on the \emph{per-day} Sharpe difference
(so CI bounds are $\sqrt{252}\approx\SqrtAnn$ times smaller than the annualized $\Delta$Sharpe beside
them; Appendix~\ref{sec:errata}). A configuration is significant iff this CI excludes zero.

\subsection{Results}

Across \SoneConfigs{} configurations (5 components, 4 combinations, 3 markets, up to 2 windows),
\textbf{\SoneSigGain{} show a significant improvement and \SoneSigLoss{} a significant loss}
(Table~\ref{tab:s1}, Figures~\ref{fig:s1matrix}--\ref{fig:s1forest}). ML exit is the clearest failure:
it loses significantly in \SoneExitSigLoss{} of its \SoneExitConfigs{} configurations and inflates
trade count \SoneExitInflLo--\SoneExitInflHi$\times$, because a per-bar continuation classifier
exits early and un-blocks crossovers that a longer-held rule-based trade would have absorbed. On the
original forex data, where the control's holdout Sharpe is \SoneForexBase{}, \SoneForexSigLoss{} of
\SoneForexConfigs{} configurations lose significantly. The closest call is meta-labeling followed by
position sizing in Indian equities: holdout Sharpe \SoneBestHoldBase{}$\to$\SoneBestHoldVar{} (CI
$[\SoneBestHoldCiLo, \SoneBestHoldCiHi]$ per day, roughly $[\SoneBestHoldCiLoAnn,
\SoneBestHoldCiHiAnn]$ annualized) and forward \SoneBestFwdBase{}$\to$\SoneBestFwdVar{} (CI
$[\SoneBestFwdCiLo, \SoneBestFwdCiHi]$) --- not significant.

\subsection{Independent re-implementation}

The released package re-implements Study 1 from scratch (Appendix~\ref{sec:repro}); running it on
the bundled data is an independent replication with three known differences (a different forex data
vendor with a flat 7\,bps cost, no sector-curation overlay, one walk-forward over the full history).
It reproduces the headline --- \SoneRepSigGain{} significant gains, ML exit significantly harmful
in every market with trade count inflated \SoneRepExitInflLo--\SoneRepExitInflHi$\times$,
\SoneRepSignAgree{} of \SoneConfigs{} configurations agreeing in sign --- but not the market-level
claims: with different forex data (control Sharpe \SoneRepForexBase{}) only \SoneRepForexSigLoss{}
forex configurations lose significantly and meta-labeling flips from \SoneMetaForexPaper{} to
\SoneMetaForexRep{}; and the Best Combined near-miss replicates on the forward window
(\SoneBestFwdDelta{} vs.\ \SoneRepBestFwd{}) but not on the holdout (\SoneBestHoldDelta{} vs.\
\SoneRepBestHold{}), where the control itself rises to \SoneRepIndiaBase{} without curation
(Table~\ref{tab:s1rep}).

Study 1 therefore leaves three questions open, which Study 2 was pre-registered to answer: does the
pattern hold (i) for other historically successful strategies, not only a JMA crossover, (ii) across a
much broader and more diverse universe, and (iii) at a second, independent freeze date, under stricter
execution assumptions and a test that respects the pairing and serial dependence of returns?
