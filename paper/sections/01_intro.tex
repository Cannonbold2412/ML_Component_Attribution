\section{Introduction}

Much ``ML for trading'' work reports one hybrid configuration --- a rule-based signal gated, filtered,
or replaced by a learned model --- against a baseline, and treats the Sharpe difference as evidence
about machine learning in trading generally. This conflates \emph{does ML help} with \emph{does ML help
at this specific decision point, with this feature set and model, holding everything else fixed}. A
hybrid pipeline has several natural insertion points --- which bars are admissible (regime filter),
whether a signal-fired trade should be taken (meta-labeling), how much to risk (position sizing), where
to enter, and when to exit --- and a single aggregate comparison cannot say which of them is responsible
for an improvement or a loss.

We introduce \textbf{Matched-Budget Component Attribution (MBCA)}: hold the data, features, model class
and hyperparameters, freeze discipline, and evaluation protocol fixed, and vary only which insertion
point a learned model replaces. We apply it twice. \textbf{Study 1} applies MBCA to one validated
strategy (a JMA crossover) in three markets and finds \SoneSigGain{} significant improvements in
\SoneConfigs{} configurations; an independent re-implementation reproduces that headline but not its
market-level claims. \textbf{Study 2} is a pre-registered test of whether Study 1 generalizes: five
historically documented strategies (JMA crossover, dual moving-average crossover, Donchian breakout,
time-series momentum, RSI(2) mean reversion) on \STwoInstruments{} instruments across \STwoMarkets{}
markets, two independent freeze dates, conservative execution, mark-to-market accounting, a paired
block-bootstrap test with false-discovery-rate control, and a battery of generalization, robustness,
and bias tests.

\textbf{Findings.} (i) ML rarely changes a historically successful rule significantly once
multiplicity is controlled: \STwoMktSigGain{} significant gains and \STwoMktSigLoss{} significant losses
in \STwoMktTests{} market-level tests. (ii) Narrowing components (meta-labeling, position sizing) are
the only ones that are directionally positive in most cells and never significantly harmful, and their
effect transfers across markets --- but it is small and replicates only partially across freezes.
(iii) Replacing components are strategy-dependent: ML entry is the most harmful component, while ML exit,
negative on average for trend-followers, is the only component that significantly improves a strategy in several
markets (RSI(2) mean reversion). (iv) Execution assumptions dominate: filling stops at the stop level
rather than at the breaching close inflates the rule-based control's Sharpe by
\STwoExecBaseStopMin--\STwoExecBaseStopMax{} and made ML exit look worse in Study 1 than it is. (v) The
controls themselves lose most of their in-sample edge out of sample.

\textbf{Contributions.} MBCA and its taxonomy of insertion points; a pre-registered, auditable Study 2 in
which data provenance, code, experiments, and every table, figure, and in-text number are generated by one
pipeline from one configuration file; the component-level evidence above; and an open-source
implementation in which a practitioner can plug in their own strategy and data.
