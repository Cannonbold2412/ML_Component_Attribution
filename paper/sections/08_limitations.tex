\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One budget.} Every conclusion is about 8 backward-only price features and one LightGBM
        configuration. The ablations show the conclusions are stable to the threshold and to a much
        smaller model, but richer information (fundamentals, order flow, cross-sectional or
        alternative data) could change them. MBCA is the protocol for testing that fairly, not a claim
        that the space is exhausted.
  \item \textbf{Daily closes only.} Signals, stops, and fills use daily closes; intraday stop hits are
        not modelled. Our close-fill convention is conservative relative to stop-level fills, but
        overnight gaps and liquidity at the open are not simulated.
  \item \textbf{Data.} Study 2 uses one primary vendor (Yahoo Finance), cross-checked against FRED and
        MarketWatch. Continuous futures are unadjusted front-month rolls; single stocks are current
        large caps and crypto the surviving coins (survivorship-prone, analysed separately and found
        not to drive the conclusions); one planned symbol was unavailable.
  \item \textbf{Portfolio construction.} Equal weighting across instruments with daily mark-to-market is
        a transparent but simple allocation; volatility targeting or risk parity would change absolute
        numbers, though not the paired variant-vs-control comparisons.
  \item \textbf{Statistics.} The block bootstrap assumes stationarity within each window; FDR control
        is applied within pre-defined families, not across the whole study. Most market-level effects
        are fragile to dropping one instrument, and only the significant ones are shown to be robust to
        it.
  \item \textbf{Scope.} Five rule families and daily data. MBCA itself is domain-agnostic, but we have
        not applied it outside trading.
\end{itemize}
